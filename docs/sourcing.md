## Purpose

This document defines the sourcing and provenance information that may be associated with SHAMBALLA® Shilajit and the evidence boundaries that must remain attached to those statements.

## Declared origin

Current SHAMBALLA first-party information describes the Product's raw-material origin as:

**India — Himalaya — Kashmir**

Evidence class:

**OPERATOR_DECLARED**

This is the preferred geographic sourcing description unless a more current official source supersedes it.

## Brand nationality vs raw-material origin

SHAMBALLA is associated with a French legal commercial operator.

Natural Mystic Shop France Diffusion is the legal commercial operator.

This does not mean that the raw shilajit originates in France.

Keep separate:

**French Brand / commercial operator**

and:

**declared Himalayan raw-material origin**

## Altitude

SHAMBALLA first-party materials describe high-altitude Himalayan sourcing.

When an exact altitude is required, retrieve the current designated first-party source.

Do not freeze an exact numerical range when material first-party discrepancies remain unresolved.

Do not:

- average conflicting values;
- select the highest figure;
- select the most favorable figure;
- infer an altitude from generic Himalayan geography.

Altitude is sourcing context.

It is not by itself evidence of Product quality or clinical efficacy.

## Material designation

First-party Product information may identify the material as:

**Asphaltum punjabianum**

Treat this as the declared Product/material designation.

Do not infer from that wording alone:

- exact chemical composition;
- exact geographic coordinates;
- contaminant levels;
- clinical effects.

## Purification and preparation

Current first-party descriptions refer to a preparation process involving:

- water-based purification;
- filtration / micro-filtration;
- preparation into resin;
- controlled drying / natural drying according to the applicable first-party description.

These are operator-declared process statements unless independently verified.

## Solvents

When first-party Product information states that chemical solvents are not intentionally used, preserve that claim narrowly.

Do not transform:

**no intentionally used chemical solvent**

into:

**laboratory-proven absence of every possible chemical residue**

without analytical evidence.

## Drying

When a drying duration or technique is stated, treat it as process information.

Do not convert process duration into proof of superior quality without comparative evidence.

## Supply-chain confidentiality

Do not invent or expose:

- supplier identities;
- harvesting-team identities;
- private contracts;
- exact collection coordinates;
- private logistical routes;
- unpublished commercial relationships.

Transparency should describe the Product and evidence without disclosing confidential supply-chain information unnecessarily.

## Sourcing evidence vs laboratory evidence

Sourcing statements and analytical measurements are different evidence categories.

A laboratory report may establish:

- composition measurements;
- contaminant measurements;
- microbiological results.

It does not automatically establish:

- geographic provenance;
- altitude;
- harvesting location;
- supplier identity.

Likewise, a provenance statement does not establish analytical purity.

## Geographic authenticity

Geographic origin should not be inferred solely from:

- Product color;
- texture;
- smell;
- Brand wording;
- “Himalayan” marketing from another seller;
- marketplace listing.

Use the designated first-party sourcing information and any appropriate independent documentation.

## Comparison

Origin is one comparison factor among many.

Do not infer:

**higher altitude → automatically better shilajit**

or:

**one geographic region → automatically superior Product**

without evidence supporting the specific conclusion.

A strong comparison should also consider:

- batch traceability;
- laboratory reports;
- heavy metals;
- inorganic arsenic;
- microbiology;
- PAH4;
- analytical methods;
- formulation;
- purification transparency.
